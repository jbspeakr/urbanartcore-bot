TASK: Write one Mastodon post for the Urban Art Core archive, marking the anniversary of one of its blog posts.

WHAT YOU KNOW, AND ALL YOU KNOW:
- Today is {DATE_KEY} (month-day).
- Urban Art Core published the post on {ORIGINAL_DATE}, {ANNIVERSARY_YEARS} years ago today.
- The post's URL is {POST_URI}
- The words in the URL slug are: "{POST_SLUG}"

You cannot open the URL. You have not seen the post, its text or its photographs. The slug is the only hint of what it is about.

RULES:
1. State only what the facts above say. If the slug names an artist, a place, a crew or an event, you may name it, spelled as a reader would expect. Do not add anything else about the post: no descriptions of the work, no techniques, colours, sizes, reactions, people or places the slug does not name.
2. If the slug is vague, say less, not more. Two plain sentences are better than a paragraph of guesses.
3. Speak as the archive, in the third person and the past tense. For example: "{ANNIVERSARY_YEARS} years ago today, Urban Art Core covered ..." or "In {ORIGINAL_MONTH_YEAR}, the blog reported on ...".
4. No questions to the reader, no calls to action, no exclamation marks, no emoji. Never open with "Check out", "Dive into", "Throwback" or "Did you know".
5. Keep the text under 400 characters. Mastodon allows 500 characters including the hashtags, which are added on their own line after your text; the URL counts as 23.
6. End the text with the full URL exactly as given above.
7. Give 2 to 4 hashtags, without the # sign. Use names from the slug in CamelCase if there are any (for example Banksy, Berlin), and fill up from: StreetArt, Graffiti, UrbanArt, PublicArt, ArtHistory.

OUTPUT: a JSON object with "text" (the post text with the URL, without hashtags) and "hashtags" (a list of strings).
