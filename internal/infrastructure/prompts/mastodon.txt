TASK: Write one Mastodon post that makes people want to open {SUBJECT} from the Urban Art Core archive.

THE STORY:
{POST_DETAILS}

{PUBLISHED} Its link is {POST_URI}

WHAT TO WRITE:
1. Talk about the story itself: the artist, crew, film, wall, city or event it is about, and what makes it worth a look. Lead with the most striking concrete detail, then use the room Mastodon gives: two to four sentences with further details from THE STORY, such as who made the work, where, how, and what it shows. Only when THE STORY says little is a shorter post right.
2. Use only facts from THE STORY above. Name the artist, crew or place from its tags when it has them. Do not invent techniques, colours, sizes, dates, quotes, reactions or names. If THE STORY says little, write a short post rather than a padded one.
3. Never write about the blog, the archive, the post or the act of publishing: no "Urban Art Core published", "the blog covered", "this post", "years ago today", "anniversary" or "throwback". You may name the year once if it helps place the work in time.
4. Write in an active, plain voice, as a curator recommending something good to a friend. THE STORY is well over a decade old, so tell it as a look back: past tense for everything it reports, the walls, shows, events and what the artists did ("painted", "showed", "ran", "lived"). Present tense only for the photos or film that can still be seen ("the photos show", "the film follows"). Never suggest that a wall still stands, a show is still on or an artist still does what THE STORY describes.
5. No questions to the reader, no emoji, no exclamation marks. Never open with "Check out", "Dive into", "Discover", "Did you know" or "Throwback".
6. Keep the text under 400 characters. Mastodon allows 500 characters including the hashtags, which are added on their own line after your text; the URL counts as 23.
7. End the text with the full URL exactly as given above.
8. Do not write hashtags, in the text or anywhere else. Three hashtags made from the story's tags are added after your text.

OUTPUT: a JSON object with "text" (the post text with the URL, without hashtags) and "claims" (every factual statement the text makes, one per entry).
